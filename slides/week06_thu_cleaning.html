The theme: none of these decisions come from the data. They come from knowing what the number means.

Three kinds of nothing

They are not the same

Value Means
NaN we don’t know
0.0 we know, and it was zero
row absent nobody measured that day

Sabino Creek, 2005–2024: 7,305 days, 2,569 of them at exactly zero.

That is a third of the record, and it is not an error. The creek is ephemeral.

What conflating them costs

Three defensible numbers, three different questions. If you dropna() your way to a “clean” series and take a mean, you have silently answered the second question while appearing to answer the first.

Your turn: which is it?

The water-level record has a 4.2-year gap. Decide what that gap is.

Hint

Manual water-level readings are made by somebody driving out with a steel tape. What does a four-year gap in that record most likely mean about the aquifer?

Solution

answer = "b"

Nothing happened to the aquifer. Somebody stopped visiting — funding, staff turnover, a locked gate. The water table went on doing whatever it was doing and nobody wrote it down.

Filling that gap invents four years of observations. Recording zeros would claim the well went dry, which is a completely different and much more alarming statement.

Drop, fill, interpolate

Three strategies, one series

Look at position 1. fillna(mean) put 46.4 between 36.5 and 41.2 — a rise that never happened. Mean-filling a trending series invents reversals.

How to choose

Situation Do
a few scattered gaps in a smooth quantity interpolate
a long gap, or a variable that jumps leave it
you need every row and the gap is short interpolate, and say so
you are about to take a mean nothing — .mean() skips NaN already

The method depends on the physics, not on the shape of the DataFrame. A water table moves smoothly and interpolates fine. A week missing during a monsoon does not — a straight line through it draws a flood that may not have happened.

Your turn: pick the strategy

Daily streamflow, and the gauge failed for nine days in August.

Hint

August is the monsoon. What is the chance that the nine missing days were a smooth ramp between their neighbours?

Solution

plan = "leave"

A nine-day gap in the monsoon could contain the largest flood of the year or nothing at all, and the two neighbours cannot tell you which. Interpolating commits you to “nothing happened”; zeros commit you to it even harder.

Leaving the gap costs you nine days of record and keeps every downstream statistic honest. NaN is a perfectly good answer — it propagates, and that is a feature.

Duplicates

The ones you’ll miss

A plain .duplicated() finds nothing, because the depths differ. But one well cannot have two water levels on one day — and that pair is exactly the problem worth finding.

Always pass subset= with the columns that ought to identify a row.

Outliers

The standard test, and what it says here

The interquartile rule: flag anything beyond \(Q_1 - 1.5\,\text{IQR}\) or \(Q_3 + 1.5\,\text{IQR}\).

Applied to twenty years of Sabino Creek: 20% of the record is an outlier.

The method hasn’t failed. It assumes roughly symmetric data, and a creek that is dry half the year is nothing of the sort. On that record, any flow at all is far from the median.

Two questions, not one

Is it unusual?

A statistical question. IQR, z-score, whatever you like.

Answer: 2,450 cfs is very unusual.

Is it wrong?

A physical question. Statistics cannot answer it.

Answer: no. It was a monsoon flood on 2 August 2006.

Drop that day as an outlier and you have deleted the single most important observation in the record for anyone sizing a culvert.

What a real error looks like

-999 is a sentinel — a fixed-width-file convention for “no data” that pandas has no way to recognise. It arrives as a number and behaves like one.

A depth to water cannot be negative. That test comes from knowing what the number means, and it is the only one that justifies deleting anything.

Your turn: which of these do you delete?

Hint 1

Two of these are physically impossible or near enough. Two are extreme but possible.

Hint 2

A depth to water of zero means the water table is at the land surface. Unusual in Tucson — but is it impossible?

Solution

delete = [2, 3]
  • 2 is a sentinel, not a measurement.
  • 3 — pH 12.4 is caustic. Not groundwater; a calibration or transcription error.
  • 1 is a real flood, and the most valuable day in the record.
  • 4 is a real possibility near a stream after a wet winter. Flag it, go and look at the field notes, but do not delete it because it surprised you.

The asymmetry matters: deleting a real observation is worse than keeping a suspect one, because a kept outlier is visible in the figure and a deleted one is gone forever.
