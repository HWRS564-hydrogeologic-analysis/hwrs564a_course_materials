Making a changed copy

New folder, new model

Put a modified model in its own folder. If you write it on top of the original, you lose the results you wanted to compare against.

Copy over what stays the same

Same grid, same boundaries. Everything is read off the loaded model m, so there are no numbers to retype and get wrong.

Change the K field

np.random.seed(42)  # For reproducibility
new_hk = np.random.normal(loc=10.0, scale=2.0, size=hk.shape)
new_hk[new_hk < 0.1] = 0.1  # Ensure minimum K value

lpf_mod = flopy.modflow.ModflowLpf(m_mod, hk=new_hk, vka=1.0, sy=0.2, ss=1e-5, laytyp=1)
  • Every cell gets its own K, drawn from a normal distribution
  • The seed means everyone in the room gets the same “random” field
  • A normal distribution can go negative and K can’t, hence the floor at 0.1
  • Two things changed at once: K is now patchy, and the average went from 5 to about 10 m/d

The new K field

Comparing

Read both head files

hds_original = flopy.utils.HeadFile(headfile_original)
head_original = hds_original.get_data()

hds_modified = flopy.utils.HeadFile(headfile_modified)
head_modified = hds_modified.get_data()

head_diff = head_modified - head_original

Both arrays are the same shape, so subtracting them just works. This is why we kept the grid identical.

Side by side

The first two look almost the same. The difference map is where you can see what changed.

How big is the change?

biggest rise in head:  +0.25 m
biggest drop in head:  -0.14 m
head range in the model: 0 to 10 m
  • We doubled the average K and the heads moved by a quarter of a meter at most
  • The two edge columns didn’t move at all. They’re constant head
  • So what did doubling K do?

Your turn: what does doubling K do?

Go back to the uniform model. Same boundaries, but K goes from 5 to 10 m/d.

Hint

Just pass in the two K values. Then look at which output changed and which didn’t.

Solution

heads_5, flow_5 = model(5.0)
heads_10, flow_10 = model(10.0)

The heads don’t change at all, and the flow doubles.

With fixed heads on both sides and uniform K, the heads are a straight line no matter what K is. K only sets how much water moves. So the small head differences on the last slide all come from K being patchy. The higher average shows up in the flow, and you won’t see it on a head map.

Check that in the budget

Total flow through each model, from the bottom of the .list files:

original:     2,778 m3/d
modified:     5,307 m3/d
ratio:         1.91

Almost double the water, with heads that barely moved. If you’d only looked at the head maps you would have missed the biggest change. Look at heads and the budget both.

Your experiment

Now you change something

Pick one:

  1. K zones: a high-K channel, or a low-K barrier across the flow
  2. A gradient: K that increases from west to east
  3. New boundary conditions: a well, or more constant-head cells
  4. Layer geometry: change the top or bottom elevations

Your turn: build a barrier

A warm-up for idea 1. Make a K array that’s 10 m/d everywhere, with a low-K wall down columns 4 and 5.

Hint

np.full(shape, value) makes the array. Slice the column axis with 4:6 to get columns 4 and 5.

Solution

my_hk = np.full((nlay, nrow, ncol), 10.0)
my_hk[:, :, 4:6] = 0.1

Pass that as hk=my_hk when you build the LPF package. Before you run it, guess what the heads will do on either side of the wall.

The recipe

  1. Make a new folder for the experiment
  2. Load the original model
  3. Create a new, empty model pointed at the new folder
  4. Copy the packages you’re keeping, change the one you’re testing
  5. Add PCG and OC, write, run, check success
  6. Subtract the original heads and plot the difference

Change one thing at a time. If you change two and the answer moves, you won’t know which one did it.
