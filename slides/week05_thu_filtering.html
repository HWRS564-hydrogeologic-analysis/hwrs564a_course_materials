Filtering makes assumptions visible

A mask is a labelled boolean sequence

deep = wells["well_depth_ft"] > 500
deep_wells = wells.loc[deep].copy()
northwest = wells.loc[
    (wells["latitude"] > 32.3)
    & (wells["longitude"] < -111.0)
].copy()
  • & means and; | means or; ~ means not
  • each condition needs parentheses
  • .copy() makes an intentional, independent subset

Missing values leave quietly

Every comparison with NaN is False. Inspect missingness before filtering or you will not know which records vanished.

Your turn: shallow northwest wells

Keep wells north of 32.3°, west of −111.0°, and shallower than 200 ft.

Hint

Join three parenthesised comparisons with &. Western longitudes are negative.

Solution

selected = wells.loc[
    (wells["latitude"] > 32.3)
    & (wells["longitude"] < -111.0)
    & (wells["depth_ft"] < 200)
].copy()

Wells A and C meet all three conditions.

New columns should say what changed

one_site["decline_ft"] = (
    one_site["depth_to_water_ft"]
    - one_site["depth_to_water_ft"].iloc[0]
)

levels["water_year"] = (
    levels["date"].dt.year
    + (levels["date"].dt.month >= 10)
)

Depth increases downward, so positive decline_ft means a falling water table. Names and units should make that convention explicit.

Grouping asks the same question repeatedly

Split, apply, combine

levels.groupby("site_no")["depth_to_water_ft"].mean()
levels.groupby("water_year")["depth_to_water_ft"].median()
levels.groupby(["site_no", "water_year"])["depth_to_water_ft"].mean()

The important decision is the key: by site, year, month, aquifer, or a category you computed.

.agg() names several summaries

site_summary = levels.groupby("site_no")["depth_to_water_ft"].agg(
    n="size",
    first="first",
    last="last",
    shallowest="min",
    deepest="max",
)

Named outputs produce a readable table in one pass. Sort by date before asking for first or last.

Your turn: monthly climatology

Average all Januaries together, all Februaries together, and so on. Keep the sample count beside the mean.

Hint

The month number is available from flow.index.month.

Solution

climatology = flow.groupby(flow.index.month)["discharge_cfs"].agg(
    mean="mean", n="size"
)

groupby(month) collapses across years. resample("ME") would retain every separate month in chronological order.

Rolling windows preserve the daily axis

Resample or roll?

monthly = q.resample("ME").mean()
rolling_30 = q.rolling(30, center=True).mean()

Resample

Fewer values at a new temporal resolution.

Rolling

One smoothed value at nearly every original date.

A centred window has honest missing ends

rolling_30 = q.rolling(30, center=True).mean()

The first and last 15 days are missing because half the window lies outside the record.

With center=False, each mean is labelled at the end of its window. That is appropriate for forecasting from the past, but it shifts historical features later by half a window.

Your turn: wettest sustained month

Hint

Use a 30-day centred window. .idxmax() returns the date of the maximum.

Solution

rolling_30 = q.rolling(30, center=True).mean()
wettest_date = rolling_30.idxmax()

A high daily maximum describes a flood. A high 30-day mean describes sustained wet conditions. They are not interchangeable statistics.

Reshape for the next operation

Long and wide store the same observations

Long

site  date        depth
A     2019-04-02   86.4
A     2020-03-18   88.1
B     2019-04-02   41.2

Compact; natural for filtering and grouping.

Wide

date         A      B
2019-04-02  86.4   41.2
2020-03-18  88.1    NaN

Convenient for comparing aligned series.

Pivot deliberately

wide = levels.pivot_table(
    index="date",
    columns="site_no",
    values="depth_to_water_ft",
)

Irregular field visits make the wide table sparse. Stay long until a plot, matrix operation, or aligned comparison actually needs wide data.

pivot_table averages duplicate site-date pairs. Check duplicates first or use pivot, which raises rather than hiding them.

Your turn: reshape and count missing cells

Hint

Dates are rows and sites are columns. Sum the boolean missing-value table twice.

Solution

wide = long.pivot(index="date", columns="site_no", values="depth_ft")
n_empty = wide.isna().sum().sum()

Five measurements occupy five of nine possible cells; four are missing.

A first time-series plot is a diagnostic

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(9, 4))
ax.plot(one_site.index, one_site["depth_to_water_ft"])
ax.invert_yaxis()

When the quantity counts downward, the axis should too. Week 8 turns this working plot into a figure worth showing people.
